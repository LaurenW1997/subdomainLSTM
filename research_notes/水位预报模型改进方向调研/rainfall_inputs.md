# Reducing rainfall-forecast error in a 1-6 h machine-learning water-level forecast, and additional hydrological inputs (state: 15 November 2026)

Reading guide. "Cited Findings" are only what was read on a source page or in a search-result extract during this session; where only an abstract or a search extract was available this is said. "Inferences" are my reasoning for the existing system (one LSTM, about 1,000 Gulf-coast gauges, 6 h RMSE 7.65 cm without rainfall, 5.95 cm with observed future rainfall, about 7.0-7.2 cm with HRRR forecast rainfall). "Gaps" lists what could not be confirmed, including things I recall but did not verify (marked "unverified recollection") - do not present those as sourced facts.

## 1. 0-6 h rainfall forecasts: skill of HRRR, time-lagged HRRR, HREF/REFS, RRFS, NBM, WPC, radar and deep-learning nowcasts; operational status in November 2026; archives usable for training

### Takeaway
RRFS (3 km, hourly, FV3 core) and its ensemble product system REFS became operational on 3 November 2026 and replaced NAM, HREF, SREF and HiresW; HRRR is NOT retired and keeps running (retirement is tied to a later RRFS upgrade to the MPAS core, no date). Only HRRR has a public archive long enough to train on (AWS, since 2014); RRFS/REFS have no supported public archive, NBM on AWS starts about 2021, MRMS on AWS starts 2022 (older MRMS only at Iowa State), and no public long HREF archive was found - so for a model trained on 2010-2022 the only forecast-rainfall "ensemble" that can be built consistently for training and real time is the time-lagged set of hourly HRRR cycles, plus radar-extrapolation nowcasts that one generates oneself from archived radar rainfall.

Skill of HRRR-type rainfall forecasts at short lead (all outside the Gulf coast unless stated)
- HRRR performance "varies across geographical domain, season, and time of day"; improvements are documented for HRRRv2, v3 and v4 - [James et al. 2022, Weather and Forecasting 37, 1397-1417](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/53529/noaa_53529_DS1.pdf) (abstract via search extract). A follow-up (Grim, Pinto and Dowell) summarises that HRRRv4 had poor skill for small, diurnally forced convective storms at the time of day of convection initiation, and that HRRRv4 and experimental RRFS under-predicted storm counts during initiation and growth - [NOAA repository document 61118](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/61118/noaa_61118_DS1.pdf) (search extract; document identity inferred from the result list).
- Displacement is the dominant error for hydrology. For 30 warm-season heavy-rain events (Upper Midwest, 2018) the centroids of 0-18 h rainfall in the HRRR ensemble and HREF were compared with river-forecast-centre rainfall: HRRR-ensemble members have less spread in centroid position than HREF and a statistically significant westward displacement in many members; HREF has no systematic direction; first-hour displacement is usually larger than that of the 0-18 h total; a correction based on the first-hour displacement helps only when made quadrant-dependent. The introduction cites Seo et al. (2018): with HRRR rainfall in a hydrologic model for the 2016 eastern Iowa floods, performance "sharply worsened after the first hour"; and Herman and Schumacher (2016): extreme rainfall is generally over-predicted by HRRR and the NAM 3 km nest. - [Kiel et al. 2022, J. Hydrometeorology 23, 1007-1021](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/60371/noaa_60371_DS1.pdf) (read from PDF)
- During the July 2025 Guadalupe River flood (Texas Hill Country) the National Water Model short-range forecasts, forced with HRRR rainfall, "considerably underpredicted the peak flow" at all four gauges with a lag; the authors note intense HRRR cells displaced by about 100 km between successive initialisations - [Baruah et al., EGUsphere preprint 2026-1847](https://egusphere.copernicus.org/preprints/2026/egusphere-2026-1847/egusphere-2026-1847.pdf) (search extract; preprint under review).
- Scale dependence (Iowa, 140 basins, about 10-40,000 km2, MRMS as reference): HRRR rainfall errors depend on spatial scale; HRRR and GFS rainfall add value to streamflow forecasts relative to a no-rain forecast, "particularly strong for basins below 1,000 km2"; skill peaks when the ratio of lead time to basin travel time is about 1 and falls systematically for ratios above 1 - [Ghimire, Krajewski and Quintero 2021, J. Hydrometeorology 22(7)](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/1811378) (abstract).
- Time-lagged HRRR versus a rapidly-updating storm-scale ensemble: for hourly 1-6 h probabilities of reflectivity of 40 dBZ or more verified against MRMS, at neighbourhood radii of about 40 km the Warn-on-Forecast System "offers little added value over HRRR-TLE [time-lagged ensemble]" - [Roberts et al., AMS 2020 handout](https://ams.confex.com/ams/2020Annual/webprogram/Handout/Paper368157/ams20-roberts-wofshrrrtl.pdf) (search extract; reflectivity, not rainfall).
- HREF rainfall-exceedance probabilities (40 km neighbourhood radius, operational method): 12 UTC HREF forecasts with regionally optimal thresholds beat the WPC Excessive Rainfall Outlook by 25-30 % in Brier skill score, but could not beat it in the southeastern US - [Dryad dataset description, doi:10.5061/dryad.7m0cfxq4j](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.7m0cfxq4j) (search extract; day-1 outlook scale, not hourly).
- Tropical cyclones: the first systematic rainfall verification of the Hurricane Analysis and Forecast System states that track-error (displacement) corrections, storm-relative coordinates and object-based verification are "more critical for TC QPF verification" - [Frontiers in Earth Science 2024, doi 10.3389/feart.2024.1417705](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/feart.2024.1417705/full) (search extract).

Radar and deep-learning nowcasts
- Radar-extrapolation nowcasts (STEPS family) "quickly lose skill after 2 hours, or even less for convective rainfall events or small radar domains"; NWP rainfall is "generally not sufficiently well captured ... in the first hours" - [EGU22-7026 abstract](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU22/EGU22-7026.html); [Imhoff et al. 2023, QJRMS](https://pure.tudelft.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/151585350/Quart_J_Royal_Meteoro_Soc_2023_Imhoff_Scale_dependent_blending_of_ensemble_rainfall_nowcasts_and_numerical_weather.pdf) (Netherlands/Belgium).
- Google MetNet (2020; inputs radar and satellite, CONUS): "outperforms ... HRRR ... for up to 7 to 8 hours of lead time" - [MetNet, arXiv 2003.12140](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2003.12140). MetNet-3 (2023): claims to "outperform the best single- and multi-member NWPs such as HRRR and ENS over the CONUS region for up to 24 hours ahead" - [MetNet-3, arXiv 2306.06079](https://pdf.arxiv.org/pdf/2306.06079) (search extract; per-lead scores not read). A 2025 satellite-based "Global MetNet" preprint compares with HRRR over the US and reports better skill at the fine neighbourhood scales relevant to nowcasting, with HRRR scoring higher only at very coarse scales - [arXiv 2510.13050](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.13050) (search extract).
- A 2026 CIROH study compares a deep-learning nowcast with optical flow, LINDA and HRRR using MRMS input, to 3 h lead, over three roughly 150 km2 watersheds in the Kansas City area, and traces the errors through NextGen hydrologic modelling; the abstract gives no numbers - [CIROH 2026 abstract, Dhital et al.](https://ciroh.ua.edu/abstracts/evaluation-of-deep-learning-based-short-term-rainfall-forecasting-for-streamflow-prediction/).

### Inferences
- A new model input must exist for 2014-2022 (training), 2024-2025 (test) and in real time. Of all products asked about, only HRRR meets this. REFS has three disqualifiers for the 1-6 h problem: no archive; cycles only every 6 h (so the newest REFS is 1-7 h old plus production latency when a forecast is made); and CONUS membership that itself contains HRRR.
- Counting from the service change notice, CONUS REFS should have (1 deterministic + 5 ensemble) x 2 cycles + 2 HRRR = 14 members; this count is my arithmetic, not a quoted number.
- Because non-synoptic HRRR cycles run to 18 h, every forecast window of 1-6 h is covered by about 12 successive hourly HRRR cycles. The archive since 2014 therefore allows a time-lagged ensemble (mean, maximum, spread, fraction of cycles above a threshold, newest-minus-older trend) for the whole training period - the one "ensemble" that needs no new data source. The WoFS comparison above suggests such a lagged ensemble is a reasonable probabilistic baseline at 40 km scales.
- The system's hourly HRRR-observation correlation of 0.3-0.5 and the absence of a gain from 15 km to 45 km boxes are consistent with the literature picture of displacement errors of tens of km up to about 100 km and of timing errors: a 45 km box is too small to absorb them. Neighbourhood maxima or percentiles over a larger radius (about 40 km radius, i.e. 80 km across, is the operational HREF convention) are the documented way to make rainfall inputs displacement-tolerant.
- RRFS v1's stated tendency to a high/heavy-rain bias argues against swapping HRRR for RRFS in a network trained on HRRR; it would need retraining, for which no archive exists. HRRR remaining operational removes the urgency.
- Deep-learning nowcasts with the best published skill (MetNet family) are not available as public gridded real-time feeds with a training archive, as far as this search found; a radar-extrapolation nowcast that one computes oneself from archived MRMS rainfall is the only nowcast that can be reproduced for training years.

### Gaps
- No source found with hourly HRRR rainfall verification (CSI/FSS/correlation by lead hour) against MRMS or Stage IV for the Gulf coast, nor for HRRR/HREF rainfall in Harvey, Imelda or Beryl; no probability-matched-mean skill for HREF in tropical cyclones.
- No quantitative RRFS-versus-HRRR or REFS-versus-HREF rainfall statistics for the operational v1 were found (NWS points to a recorded webinar that was not viewed).
- Not searched/confirmed: NowcastNet, DGMR and PySTEPS skill horizons with numbers (unverified recollection: DGMR targets 5-90 min, NowcastNet up to 3 h); ECMWF products for 0-6 h; WPC QPF archive location and start date; any operational MRMS/FLASH rainfall-nowcast product.
- Unverified recollections that should be checked before use: MRMS became operational in autumn 2014 and its rainfall products were renamed with MRMS v12 in October 2020; HRRR version dates (v1 Sept 2014, v2 Aug 2016, v3 Jul 2018, v4 Dec 2020), which make the HRRR archive inhomogeneous; a Google Cloud copy of the HRRR archive; NBM on AWS possibly starting in 2020 rather than 2021; completeness of the 2014-2016 HRRR archive.
- Whether NOAA has started mirroring operational RRFS/REFS files to the AWS bucket after 3 Nov 2026 was not checked.

## 2. Blending radar nowcast (0-2 h) with NWP (2-6 h): documented gains for flash-flood forecasting

### Takeaway
Blending is the standard remedy for the weak first hours of NWP rainfall, and published tests show the blend tracks the nowcast for 1-2 h and then relaxes to the NWP, performing better than or on par with the better single product; quantified hydrological gains exist for radar nowcasts alone (average warning lead of roughly 2-3.5 h over a no-rain benchmark in 12 Dutch catchments), but no study was found that quantifies the gain of a blend over HRRR alone for US flash-flood stage forecasts.

### Cited Findings
- Dutch lowland catchments (supporting evidence from elsewhere): 659 events, 12 catchments, four open-source nowcasting algorithms fed to a hydrologic model. Relative to a zero-rain benchmark, threshold exceedances were forecast on average 223 min (Eulerian persistence), 196 and 213 min (two rainymotion variants), 119 min (pysteps deterministic) and 143 min (pysteps probabilistic) in advance; persistence's lead comes with a high false-alarm ratio and inconsistent forecasts; the dense-rotation rainymotion and both pysteps variants give the best discharge forecasts; errors grow with rainfall intensity and spatial variability, and faster when the groundwater table is shallow; all methods beat issuing no rainfall forecast; the main recommendation is to improve forecast rainfall volumes - [Imhoff et al. 2022, Water Resources Research](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:156d74f6-44f7-4c37-a1c7-6ac065e3edbc) (abstract via search extract).
- Scale-dependent (cascade-level) blending of ensemble nowcasts with NWP in pysteps: weights come from the recent skill of each component and converge to climatological values; the blend "predominantly performs similarly or better than only nowcasting and adds value compared with NWP for the first hours of the forecast"; the advantage over simple linear blending shrinks for catchment-average sums - [Imhoff et al. 2023, QJRMS](https://pure.tudelft.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/151585350/Quart_J_Royal_Meteoro_Soc_2023_Imhoff_Scale_dependent_blending_of_ensemble_rainfall_nowcasts_and_numerical_weather.pdf) (search extract).
- Operational test in Dutch water authorities (15 Jul-15 Aug 2023): the blend "closely tracks the nowcast quality during the initial 1 to 2 hours", then moves to NWP; "either better or on par with the best individual product"; run time 3.4 min deterministic, 12.3 min for 10 members on 4 cores for a 12 h forecast - [EGU24-5849 abstract](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU24/EGU24-5849.html).
- Liguria, Italy (small steep basins): nowcast-only rainfall "lead[s] to an overestimation of the discharge", while blended fields "clearly improve the discharge forecast"; in larger basins the benefit lasts to longer leads - [Silvestro, RealPEP workshop 2020](https://indico.kit.edu/event/883/contributions/7471/attachments/3808/5594/Silvestro_FFP_RealPEP_2020_Oct7.pdf) (presentation); related peer-reviewed paper on flash-flood forecasting at 2-8 h lead in small catchments, not read: [HESS 23, 3823, 2019](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/23/3823/2019/hess-23-3823-2019.pdf).
- With HRRR rainfall in a hydrologic model (Iowa 2016), performance "sharply worsened after the first hour", with some recovery at hours five and six - Seo et al. 2018 as cited in [Kiel et al. 2022](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/60371/noaa_60371_DS1.pdf).

### Inferences
- For urban creeks with 1-6 h response, the rain of the first 1-3 h of the forecast window largely determines stage at 3-6 h; that is the window where extrapolation of observed radar rainfall is strongest and HRRR weakest. A network can do the "blending" itself if it is given both an extrapolation nowcast (hours 1-2 or 1-3) and HRRR (hours 1-6) as separate inputs; the literature's skill-dependent weights are then learned rather than prescribed.
- Expected benefit is largest for organised, advecting rain (squall lines, tropical-cyclone rainbands) and smallest for convection that initiates inside the window, which extrapolation cannot see.

### Gaps
- No US Gulf-coast or Houston study quantifying stage/discharge error with nowcast-NWP blend versus HRRR alone was found.
- No evidence was found on blended rainfall as an input to a machine-learning (rather than process-based) stage model.

## 3. Making the network robust to rainfall-forecast error (ensemble inputs, mixed training, time-lagged cycles, neighbourhood rainfall, hindcast-forecast designs)

### Takeaway
The best-documented design is the hindcast/forecast (encoder-decoder) LSTM of Google's flood model, which feeds real forecast-product rainfall to a separate forecast LSTM, tolerates missing sources through substitution plus flags, and predicts a probability distribution rather than one value; but I found no published experiment that measures how much of the perfect-forecast gain is recovered by ensemble statistics, time-lagged cycles, neighbourhood rainfall or mixed observed/forecast training in an hourly model - this is an open question the existing system could answer itself.

### Cited Findings
- Google global flood model (daily, 7-day horizon): two LSTMs - an encoder over 365 days of past meteorological inputs and a separate decoder over the 7-day horizon "with inputs from meteorological forecasts"; hidden size 256 in both; cell state handed over through a linear layer and hidden state through a tanh layer; inputs ECMWF IFS HRES forecasts, ERA5-Land, NOAA CPC and NASA IMERG early-run precipitation, all "area-weighted" over each gauge's upstream basin, plus HydroATLAS attributes; missing inputs handled either by substitution from a similar source ("HRES data was imputed with ERA5-Land data") or by a mean value plus a binary flag; no streamflow input (deliberately, for ungauged use); output = parameters of an asymmetric-Laplacian (mixture) distribution per lead time, trained with negative log-likelihood; three separately trained models averaged; 5,680 training gauges - [Nearing et al., arXiv 2307.16104v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.16104v4); published as [Nearing et al. 2024, Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07145-1). Headline: 5-day-lead reliability similar to or better than GloFAS nowcasts (same sources).
- Multi-timescale LSTM (hourly and daily in one model; CAMELS-US): long-past inputs are processed at coarse resolution and recent ones hourly, and different input variables can be used at different timescales - "especially relevant to operational applications where the lead time of meteorological forcings depends on their temporal resolution" - [Gauch et al. 2021, HESS 25, 2045](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/25/2045/2021/).
- A 2026 preprint proposes a multi-frequency "masked-forecasting" LSTM for hourly streamflow on CAMELS-DE-1h, and a 2026 HESS paper by the same group is described as unifying multi-frequency modelling, missing-data handling and real-time data assimilation for hourly forecasting - [EGUsphere preprint 2026-4885](https://egusphere.copernicus.org/preprints/2026/egusphere-2026-4885/egusphere-2026-4885.pdf) (search extract only; results not read).
- Predicting a distribution instead of a point (daily, 196 Swiss catchments): mean CRPS 0.50 (countable mixture of asymmetric Laplacians), 0.53 (distributional regression), 0.56 (Bernstein quantile network); NSE 0.87 deterministic versus 0.86/0.84/0.83; for the top 0.05 % of flows the deterministic LSTM underestimates about 97 % of events while about 83 % fall within the mixture model's 99th percentile - [Baste et al. 2026, HESS 30, 6339-6358](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/30/6339/2026/).
- Train on observations, run on forecasts: an LSTM trained on reanalysis/observations and run with ECMWF forecasts to 10 days at 6-hourly steps (western US), which also built training series from archived IFS forecasts - [Hunt et al. 2022, HESS 26, 5449](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/26/5449/2022/) (search extract; no head-to-head numbers extracted); a Canadian study trained LSTMs on observed data with forecast inputs at run time and a separate model per lead time - [J. Hydrology 2023, S0022169423013227](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022169423013227) (search extract).
- Ensemble forcing: a standalone LSTM trained on observed forcings and then driven by GEFS reforecast ensemble members, and an LSTM trained on the residuals of a hydrologic model's raw ensemble, separately per lead time and member (daily, US) - [arXiv 2106.09547](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.09547). Hourly hybrid (Yantan, China): ECMWF ensemble plus land-surface model cut errors by 6 % versus climatological-forcing ensembles over 7 days, and an LSTM correction cut deterministic error by a further 6 % in the first 72 h; an LSTM using only past observations lost skill sharply after 24 h - [HESS 26, 265, 2022](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/26/265/2022/hess-26-265-2022.pdf) (search extract).
- "Perfect rainfall" benchmark experiments (observed rain substituted for forecast rain to bound the possible gain) are used in a cascade-LSTM study on the Yangtze (daily) - [ProQuest record](https://www.proquest.com/scholarly-journals/daily-streamflow-forecasts-based-on-cascade-long/docview/2791750962/se-2) (search extract).
- Displacement-aware ensembles: a streamflow-ensemble approach that spatially shifts the forecast rainfall to represent location uncertainty (Carlberg et al. 2020) is discussed, with the suggestion to weight the shifted members by the displacement error seen in the first hour of rain - [Kiel et al. 2022](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/60371/noaa_60371_DS1.pdf). A search extract attributed to the same group reports that shifted rainfall in the HRRR ensemble gave better probabilistic flood skill, that flood-peak timing errors averaged about five hours early, and that larger watersheds scored better - [NOAA repository document 60370](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/60370/noaa_60370_DS1.pdf) (not opened; attribution uncertain).
- Operational neighbourhood convention: HREF rainfall-exceedance probabilities use a 40 km neighbourhood radius - [Dryad dataset description](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.7m0cfxq4j); "neighbourhood maximum ensemble probability" is the probability of exceedance anywhere within the neighbourhood - [NOAA GSL publication 186](https://publications.gsl.noaa.gov/articles/186) (search extract).
- Houston precedent for future-rain input: a flood-status classifier on Harris County's 175 channel sensors uses "rainfall projected during the future 6 hours" among its inputs - [arXiv 2006.09201](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.09201) (search extract).

### Inferences
- The existing system already does the single most important thing (training on the forecast product it will receive). What the Google design adds and the system lacks: (a) a predicted distribution, so that rainfall uncertainty turns into spread rather than damped means; (b) several rainfall sources side by side with missing-flags, so a new source can be added for the years it exists; (c) basin-area rainfall.
- Candidate inputs that can be built for 2014 onward from the HRRR archive alone, ranked by how directly the cited literature supports them: (1) time-lagged statistics over the last 6-12 HRRR cycles per forecast hour block; (2) neighbourhood statistics (mean, 90th percentile, maximum within about 40 km radius, and box-mean at the catchment) to tolerate displacement; (3) the HRRR error known at forecast time - HRRR rain for the past 1-3 h from earlier cycles minus observed rain for the same hours - which is the machine-learning analogue of Kiel et al.'s first-hour displacement correction and lets the network discount a cycle that is already wrong; (4) an extrapolation nowcast for hours 1-2.
- Training on a random mixture of observed and forecast future rain is not supported by any study found; with a network that sees which source it is given (flag), it would teach the rainfall-to-stage response from clean data while keeping calibration for forecast rain. Treat as an experiment, not as an evidence-based recommendation.
- The persistent positive bias at 34 stations in hurricanes that is partly traced to HRRR rainfall matches the cited over-prediction of extreme rainfall by HRRR (Herman and Schumacher 2016, via Kiel et al.); an explicit HRRR-rain-minus-observed-rain feature for the preceding hours is the most direct handle on it.
- An upper bound is already known for the system: 7.65 to 5.95 cm. Any of the tricks above can be scored by the fraction of that 1.7 cm recovered; no literature value exists to compare against.

### Gaps
- No study found that feeds ensemble members or ensemble statistics of rainfall into an hourly LSTM and reports the gain relative to a deterministic forecast and to observed rainfall.
- No study found on mixed observed/forecast-rain training, input-noise augmentation of rainfall, or multi-cycle inputs for hourly stage forecasting.
- Nearing et al.'s text does not state explicitly whether archived real HRES forecasts were used throughout training (the decoder is described as taking forecasts; substitution with ERA5-Land where HRES is missing implies real forecasts where available). The hourly Google/Kratzert-group papers (2026) were not read in full.

## 4. Catchment-average rainfall versus a fixed box at the gauge

### Takeaway
Large-sample LSTM practice (Google, CAMELS-based studies) uses rainfall averaged over the contributing basin, and spatially distributed rainfall has been reported to improve on basin-mean rainfall; I found no head-to-head test of basin-average versus a fixed gauge-centred box for small urban basins, so the benefit for Houston bayous is plausible but unquantified.

### Cited Findings
- In the Google global model all meteorological inputs are "area-weighted" over each gauge's upstream basin - [Nearing et al., arXiv 2307.16104v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.16104v4).
- A HESS study on 10 CAMELS catchments found that rainfall with spatial information improves LSTM performance compared with models driven by mean rainfall, for per-catchment and regional training - [HESS 26, 2387, 2022](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/26/2387/2022/) (search extract; article identity from the result list, not opened). A 2025 HESS paper argues lumped inputs can be a reasonable assumption for small catchments but lose within-catchment covariance - [HESS 29, 5233, 2025](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/29/5233/2025/hess-29-5233-2025.pdf) (search extract).
- Urban watershed at 15-minute resolution with rainfall as the only input (LSTM): performance was maintained when a selected subset of rain gauges replaced all gauges - [arXiv 2002.02568](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.02568.pdf) (search extract; I believe this is the Brays Bayou, Houston study but did not confirm).
- Forecast-rainfall errors and the value of forecast rainfall are scale dependent, largest value below 1,000 km2, best when lead time is about equal to basin travel time - [Ghimire et al. 2021](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/1811378).
- Small Iowa urban-rural watersheds: the space-time resolution of radar rainfall changed simulated streamflow differently by model and storm - [Atmosphere, July 2020](https://doaj.org/article/63cbb6f64c1a4c628118afa61bea5b19) (search extract).

### Inferences
- A 15 km box at the gauge is a poor proxy in two opposite cases present in the system: (a) river gauges with basins of hundreds to thousands of km2, where the box covers a few per cent of the contributing area and misses upstream rain entirely (there the past upstream discharge already carries the information, which may be why past rain adds little); (b) urban creeks whose 20-300 km2 catchment lies upstream on one side of the gauge, so a centred box is half outside the catchment.
- For forecast rain the picture differs: displacement errors of tens of km mean that a tight catchment mask on HRRR is not better than a box; the sensible combination is catchment-average for observed (past) rain and neighbourhood/catchment-buffer statistics for forecast rain.
- Ghimire et al.'s travel-time result gives a rule for which stations can gain from 6 h forecast rain at all: those with response time near or below 6 h (the urban creeks); for slower rivers the gain should come from catchment-average observed rain and upstream stages instead.

### Gaps
- No direct comparison of NHDPlus/StreamStats/HUC12 basin-average rainfall against a gauge-centred box for small urban basins was found.
- Not checked: availability of contributing-area polygons for all 1,000 gauges (tidal and canal gauges have no well-defined basin).

## 5. Urban flash-flood machine-learning forecasting (Houston and other cities); the Harris County network

### Takeaway
Published Houston studies use the Harris County Flood Control District network (over 350 rain and stage gauges, event-driven reporting) with dense rain-gauge inputs and short horizons, but none that I found reports a continuous 1-6 h stage RMSE comparable to the system's 10.7 cm; the district offers history from each station's installation date through a web interface and bulk 5-minute files, with no documented public API.

### Cited Findings
- Harris County Flood Warning System: "over 350 gage stations"; rain sensors report each 0.04 inch, water-level sensors each 0.10 ft change; map data delayed by about five minutes; historical rainfall and water level available from each station's installation date; bulk historical datasets in 5-minute increments in the "Document Library"; per-station "Export to Excel"; the FAQ mentions no API; the district and USGS share shelters and level sensors at about 50 sites - [Harris County FWS FAQ](https://www.harriscountyfws.org/faqs).
- Unofficial access routes: a community scraper for historical data - [HouFloodScraper on GitHub](https://github.com/dcbark01/HouFloodScraper); ArcGIS REST layers for the regional gauges listed in a Houston-Galveston Area Council document (map layers; time series not confirmed) - [H-GAC document](https://www.h-gac.com/getmedia/11b81848-4088-4708-b504-366e6fee2ab1/FloodWarningSystem). Newer district radar sensors deliver continuous level, velocity and discharge - [KISTERS case study](https://www.kisters.net/success-story/initiating-ai-flow-data-collection-to-better-forecast-flood-risk/).
- Houston studies: (a) hybrid FastGRNN-FCN on 175 Harris County channel sensors - a classifier of channel flooding status, inputs include forecast rain for the next 6 h - [arXiv 2006.09201](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.09201); (b) XGBoost for White Oak Bayou stage at 5-minute interval with several look-ahead windows - [Water 2022, 14(5), 747](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4441/14/5/747) (publisher page blocked; errors not confirmed); (c) LSTM rainfall-runoff at 15-minute resolution for Brays Bayou - [Neural Computing and Applications 2020, s00521-020-05010-6](https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/doi/10.1007/s00521-020-05010-6) (search extract).
- Other urban evidence: a 2026 study in a flood-prone urban basin with 10-minute rain and flow and an LSTM for 1-12 h leads reports that choosing inputs by inter-station lag times improved accuracy (no soil moisture) - [J. Hydroinformatics 2026, doi 10.2166/hydro.2026.180](https://doi.org/10.2166/hydro.2026.180) (search extract). Google's urban flash-flood model (announced 12 Mar 2026): LSTM, 20 km grid, up to 24 h, inputs IMERG, CPC, ECMWF HRES and an AI global weather model plus static attributes, labels from news reports; at the same 20 km/24 h aggregation, NWS flash-flood warnings have recall 22 % and precision 44 % - [Google Research blog](https://research.google/blog/protecting-cities-with-ai-driven-flash-flood-forecasting/).
- National hourly benchmark: a physics-embedded learned model reaches median hourly NSE 0.683 at 2,831 gauges versus 0.461 for the National Water Model v3.0, and cuts flood-peak timing errors from 7-8 h to 4.5-6 h (simulation with observed forcing; flash-flood basins not broken out) - [Yang et al. 2026, arXiv 2609.06794](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06794).

### Inferences
- The district's rain gauges (about 5-minute, event-driven, dense over exactly the 71 urban-creek catchments) are an independent rainfall observation for the past window and a check on MRMS in real time; they do not help with the future 6 h, which is where the system's gain comes from. Their main use would be sub-hourly intensity for the last 1-2 h and upstream stage gauges on the same bayou (the district has many more stage gauges than the 71 USGS ones) as extra upstream inputs - upstream stage is an input that needs no rainfall forecast at all for the first hours.
- Google's urban product (20 km, 24 h, yes/no) is not a competitor or an input for hourly stage at a gauge.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed 1-6 h stage RMSE for Houston bayous was confirmed in this session; the White Oak Bayou paper's numbers could not be read.
- Not found: studies for Austin, Dallas, Charlotte, Tokyo or Seoul with comparable hourly stage errors (not searched specifically).
- Whether the Flood Warning System's bulk files cover 2010-2022 for most stations, the licence, and whether an undocumented JSON endpoint is stable enough for real-time use were not verified.

## 6. Antecedent wetness (SMAP, NLDAS/NWM soil moisture, antecedent precipitation index)

### Takeaway
I found no study that isolates the benefit of soil-moisture inputs for 1-6 h stage forecasts once recent water level and 72 h of rain are already inputs; the available evidence says that assimilating recent discharge captures most of the state information (daily median NSE 0.74 to 0.86 in the US), while process-model studies indicate that initial states and wetness control how rainfall errors translate into flow errors.

### Cited Findings
- Feeding recent discharge observations to an LSTM ("data integration") raised the median one-day-ahead NSE across CONUS basins from 0.74 to 0.86; the gain is largest where flow is strongly autocorrelated, and even with it, arid basins with one-day flash peaks remain poor - [Feng, Fang and Shen 2020, arXiv 1912.08949](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.08949v2) (search extract). In the same group's near-real-time SMAP forecasting work, integrating the latest observations "removed the autocorrelated effects of forcing errors" - [OSTI record 1602597](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/1602597) (search extract; link from the result list, not opened).
- Updating a physically based model's state with SMAP soil moisture (native and downscaled) "shows promising potential for improving short term flood forecasting" - [EGU22-3318 abstract](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU22/EGU22-3318.html) (conference abstract, data assimilation, not an ML input).
- In 12 Dutch catchments the discharge-forecast error caused by nowcast rainfall grows faster with rain intensity when the groundwater table is shallow (wet state) - [Imhoff et al. 2022](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:156d74f6-44f7-4c37-a1c7-6ac065e3edbc).
- For National Water Model short-range forecasts at small flash-flood-prone basins, the model states used to initialise the forecast are identified as the main limit on high-flow prediction, especially without streamflow assimilation - [Quintero, Krajewski and Vergara 2026, J. Hydrometeorology 27(7), 1147-1161](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Evaluation-of-Short-Range-Streamflow-Forecasts-at/9985183138002771).
- Google's urban flash-flood model uses soil absorption as a static attribute, not dynamic soil moisture - [Google Research blog](https://research.google/blog/protecting-cities-with-ai-driven-flash-flood-forecasting/).

### Inferences
- In the existing system the 72 h rain history plus the current water level (and its recent change) already encode wetness for most rivers; the expected extra value of soil moisture is confined to flashy creeks that start from base level, where the water level before the storm says nothing about infiltration capacity. For heavily paved Houston catchments the runoff coefficient is high regardless of wetness, which further limits the gain there; the more promising places are the less urbanised small rivers.
- A cheap test that needs no new data source: add 7-, 14- and 30-day antecedent rain totals from the AORC/MRMS series already held (the 72 h window cannot represent a wet fortnight). If that gives nothing, satellite or model soil moisture is unlikely to.
- SMAP surface retrievals (2-3 day revisit, about 9-36 km) are a poor match to hourly 20-300 km2 catchments; model-based hourly products (NLDAS, National Water Model land states) match better in time and can be reproduced for training years.

### Gaps
- No ablation found of SMAP, SMAP Level-4, NLDAS or National Water Model soil moisture, or of an antecedent precipitation index, in an hourly autoregressive ML stage/flow forecast.
- Archive spans and latency of SMAP Level-4 and NLDAS were not checked in this session (unverified recollection: SMAP from April 2015; SMAP Level-4 latency about 2-3 days; NLDAS-2 latency several days, so real-time use would need the National Water Model analysis instead).

## 7. National Water Model short-range forecasts and analysis as an input or as something to post-process

### Takeaway
Post-processing the National Water Model with an LSTM beats the model itself (daily: median NSE 0.73-0.75 versus 0.62), but an LSTM with forcing alone did about as well, and the model's own short-range skill at small flashy basins collapses within hours (median probability of detecting high flows 0.8 at hour 1 to zero after hour 7 even with streamflow assimilation); the operational output is kept only as a rolling four-week archive on AWS while the retrospective run (1979-2023) is not a forecast, so a training set of real short-range forecasts back to 2015-2020 could not be confirmed.

### Cited Findings
- LSTM post-processing of the National Water Model, 531 CONUS basins, daily: post-processors reach median NSE 0.73-0.75 versus 0.62 for the model; a standalone LSTM using only atmospheric forcing performed about as well as the post-processors, pointing to the routing scheme as a priority for improvement - [Frame et al. 2021, JAWRA (Google Research page)](https://research.google/pubs/post-processing-the-national-water-model-with-long-short-term-memory-networks-for-streamflow-predictions-and-model-diagnostics/) (search extract).
- Hourly sequence-to-sequence LSTM correcting National Water Model short-range runoff errors at 1-18 h lead in the Russian River basin (California), using observed precipitation and upstream gauge errors; percent bias and RMSE reduced after post-processing (numbers not seen) - [Han and Morrison, record at NAPSTIC](https://search.napstic.cn/literature/periodical/fed782fe2d603a70393775d45b550b73) (search extract).
- Verification of operational short-range forecasts 2019-2024 against hourly USGS data at 7,500 locations: with streamflow assimilation the median probability of detection of high flows at small flash-flood-prone basins falls from 0.8 at forecast hour 1 to zero after 7 h; without assimilation from 0.1 to zero after 5 h; median KGE without assimilation is -0.6 (below the -0.41 reference); HRRR rainfall errors reduce KGE at headwater basins - [Quintero, Krajewski and Vergara 2026](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Evaluation-of-Short-Range-Streamflow-Forecasts-at/9985183138002771).
- Texas: evaluation of versions 2.1 and 3.0 at 610 USGS gauges found continuing difficulty with both magnitude and timing of peaks - [DOAJ record](https://library.kab.ac.ug/Record/doaj-art-53785e5ca02d414cb7dbc8b8adb38d43/Description) (search extract); Guadalupe July 2025: short-range forecasts considerably under-predicted peaks with a lag at all four gauges - [Baruah et al., EGUsphere 2026-1847](https://egusphere.copernicus.org/preprints/2026/egusphere-2026-1847/egusphere-2026-1847.pdf).
- Hourly retrospective skill: National Water Model v3.0 median hourly NSE 0.461 at 2,831 gauges, flood-peak timing errors 7-8 h - [Yang et al. 2026, arXiv 2609.06794](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06794).
- Configuration and archives: the short-range forecast "cycles hourly and produces hourly deterministic forecasts of streamflow and hydrologic states out to 18 hours", forced by HRRR and RAP; the AWS bucket `noaa-nwm-pds` is "a rolling four week archive" - [AWS Registry, operational NWM](https://registry.opendata.aws/noaa-nwm-pds/). Retrospective v3.0: February 1979-January 2023, NetCDF and Zarr, bucket `noaa-nwm-retrospective-3-0-pds`; v2.1 also on Google Cloud (`national-water-model-v2-1`) - [AWS Registry, NWM retrospective](https://registry.opendata.aws/nwm-archive/); [CUAHSI blog on the 42-year retrospective](https://cuahsi.org/community/news/big-data-dreaming-a-42-year-conus-hydrologic-retrospective).

### Inferences
- As an input for 1-6 h stage at gauged sites, the short-range streamflow forecast inherits the same HRRR rainfall error the system already suffers from, adds timing errors of several hours, and at gauges with assimilation mostly restates the observed flow the LSTM already has. Frame et al.'s result (forcing-only LSTM about equal to post-processor) says the hydrologic model adds little beyond its forcing for an LSTM. Expected gain: low.
- The useful part is likely indirect: (a) the analysis-cycle land states (soil saturation, ponded water) as an hourly wetness input reproducible for training from the retrospective run - with the caveat that retrospective and operational analysis differ in forcing (AORC versus MRMS/HRRR-RAP) and in assimilation, i.e. a train/real-time mismatch; (b) streamflow at ungauged upstream reaches or tributaries as an "upstream gauge" surrogate for river stations.
- Latency (not verified here) matters: an hourly cycle's output arriving 1-2 h after its nominal time would consume a third of the 6 h window.

### Gaps
- An archive of operational short-range forecasts or analyses before the rolling four weeks was not confirmed (unverified recollection: a Google Cloud bucket `national-water-model` holds operational output from about 2018 onward; check before planning).
- No study found that uses National Water Model output as an input feature of an ML stage model at hourly 1-6 h leads on the Gulf coast; Han and Morrison's numbers and venue were not confirmed.
- Production latency of short-range and analysis cycles was not confirmed.

## 8. Regulated gauges: real-time reservoir release, gate and pump data, and studies that used operations data

### Takeaway
USACE publishes water-management time series (including reservoir outflow parameters) through the public, unauthenticated CWMS Data API, and Texas river authorities publish releases on web portals without documented APIs; studies outside the region show that adding reservoir outflow or gate/pump flows to recurrent networks is feasible (about 0.2 m RMSE at 1 h below a Taiwanese reservoir), but I found no quantified ablation of operations inputs and no such study for the Gulf coast.

### Cited Findings
- CWMS Data API: REST API "providing public access to USACE water management data", readable without authentication; `/timeseries` and `/timeseries/recent` endpoints; production server `cwms-data.usace.army.mil` - [CWMS Data API documentation](https://cwms-data-api.readthedocs.io/latest/genindex.html) (search extract). An OGC-EDR proxy ("USACE Access2Water") lists locations such as "Barker Res nr Addicks, TX" (location code 29049) and parameters including Outflow and Surcharge Release in cfs - [Access2Water collection](https://api.wwdh.internetofwater.app/collections/usace-edr?f=html); [item 29049](https://api.wwdh.internetofwater.app/collections/usace-edr/items/29049) (search extract).
- Addicks and Barker are USACE flood-control reservoirs operated together to prevent flooding of Buffalo Bayou in Houston, normally empty - [Texas Water Development Board, Addicks](https://www.twdb.texas.gov/surfacewater/rivers/reservoirs/addicks/index.asp).
- Texas aggregators and authorities: Water Data for Texas polls providers only about every six hours - [Water Data for Texas methodology](https://waterdatafortexas.org/reservoirs/methodology); LCRA Hydromet shows lake levels, previous-day releases and a flood-operations report with dam discharge - [LCRA river report](https://hydromet.lcra.org/riverreport), [LCRA flood operations report](https://hydromet.lcra.org/floodstatus); the San Jacinto River Authority publishes Lake Conroe level and release rate through a OneRain Contrail site (sanjacinto.onerain.com) - [SJRA document](https://www.sjra.net/wp-content/uploads/2015/01/Update-on-Rainfall-and-River-Flow.pdf) and search extract. No documented public API was found for these.
- Louisiana: USGS operates a gauge at the Bonnet Carre Spillway on the Mississippi (USGS-07374370) - [USGS monitoring location](https://waterdata.usgs.gov/monitoring-location/USGS-07374370/); older New Orleans District pages point to RiverGages.com for near-real-time stages (pages from about 2008-2016; current gate feeds not confirmed) - [archived New Orleans District water-control page](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20090109021317/http:/www.mvn.usace.army.mil/eng/edhd/wcontrol/wcmain.asp).
- Studies (all outside the region): Taiwan - adding reservoir outflow to RNN/SVM/LSTM water-level forecasts downstream; 1 h forecasts have correlation above 0.97 and RMSE about 0.2 m, declining at longer horizons - [EGU23-17521 abstract](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU23/EGU23-17521.html). South Florida (Miami River) - MLP/RNN/CNN/LSTM stage prediction using recent measurements plus covariates "for which predictions are typically available for the near future" (the search extract names gate and pump flows and tide), reported "significantly better" than HEC-RAS with over 500x speed-up - [Shi et al., arXiv 2306.15907](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15907). CNN-LSTM for levels downstream of a dam - [ASCE J. Water Resources Planning and Management, 0001432](https://ascelibrary.com/doi/10.1061/%28ASCE%29WR.1943-5452.0001432) (search extract). Urban river levels with an underground regulating reservoir's gate operations (Japan) - [Journal of JSCE 13(2)](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/journalofjsce/13/2/13_24-16107/_article/-char/en) (search extract).

### Inferences
- Operations are a "known future input" problem like rainfall: past releases are observable, future releases are decisions. For the handful of gauges directly below Addicks/Barker, Lake Conroe, Lake Houston or navigation locks, the current release (and its last change) is information no other input carries; for 1-6 h leads, persistence of the current gate setting is usually a good "forecast" of the operation, which makes this input far less error-prone than rainfall.
- A practical first step is diagnostic rather than modelling: list the stations whose largest errors coincide with release changes (the system's per-station RMSE can show this), then check CWMS for a matching outflow series before building any ingest.
- In a single global LSTM, an operations input that exists for perhaps 10-30 of 1,000 gauges needs a missing-flag design (as in the Google model) so it does not disturb the rest.

### Gaps
- Not verified: which Galveston, Fort Worth and New Orleans District locations expose outflow or gate-opening series in CWMS, their history length (for training back to 2010) and update interval; whether Harris County or coastal Louisiana pump-station operations are published anywhere in real time.
- BRA and TRA data services were not checked. No quantified ablation (with versus without operations input) was found in any study; the South Florida paper's error values were not read.
